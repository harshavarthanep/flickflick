Keys

Seven keys. That is the whole manual.

Each one does one thing, and none of them replaces a shortcut your browser already has. Change any of them whenever you like.

AltQ

Flick to your last tab. Press it again to flick back.

Hold Alt tap Q Q

Pick any recent tab. Your tabs appear with pictures. Let go, or click one, to open it.

Click a link

Peek with Glance. The link opens in a floating window. Open it full size or side by side without reloading, or press Esc twice to close it.

AltK

Open the command bar. Find any tab, bookmark or page from your history, do quick maths, or type “remind me to call Mom at 6”.

AltL

Copy the link, with the trackers already removed.

AltP

Picture in picture for the video on the page.

Alt/

See every shortcut on any page, any time.

On a Mac, Alt is the Option key (⌥).

Questions

Good to know.

Why Alt+Q and not Ctrl+Tab?

Chrome keeps Ctrl+Tab for itself and lets no extension change it. Alt+Q sits right under your fingers, works the same way, and you can pick any other key on your browser’s shortcuts page.

Which browsers does it work in?

Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi, Arc and Opera. Opera has no side panel, so vertical tabs are not available there; everything else works.

Why does it ask to work on all sites?

Only so it can show the switcher on the page you are on, open Glance when you click a link, remember your volume for each site, and show the pet. It reads no page content and sends nothing anywhere. It asks once, and you can say no.

Will it slow my browser down?

No. It does almost nothing until you press a key. Mouse movement and background tabs cost nothing, and every feature can be switched off.

How do I learn it?

A one-minute tour opens when you install it. Press Alt+/ on any page to see every shortcut, and take the tour again any time from Settings.

Is it free?

Yes, with no ads and nothing to sign up for.
